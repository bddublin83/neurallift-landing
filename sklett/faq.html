Section 1: Understanding Therapeutic Ketosis

1. What exactly IS therapeutic ketosis, and how is it different from regular "keto"?

Therapeutic ketosis is achieving and maintaining blood β-hydroxybutyrate (BHB) levels ≥4.0 mmol/L—the threshold where significant neurological and metabolic benefits occur.

Comparison:
  • Regular keto diet: 1.5–2.5 mmol/L (mild ketosis)
  • Therapeutic ketosis (SKLeTT): 4.0–6.0 mmol/L
  • Multi-day fasting: 4.0–8.0 mmol/L

Most popular "keto" programs focus on weight loss and general health, targeting mild ketosis sufficient for fat adaptation. SKLeTT targets the higher range where BHB activates specific molecular pathways:

  • SIRT1/AMPK/PGC-1α activation → mitochondrial biogenesis
  • HDAC inhibition → BDNF up-regulation (neuroplasticity)
  • NLRP3 inflammasome inhibition → reduced neuroinflammation

Why it matters: These pathways show dose-dependent responses. At 1.5 mmol/L, you get some benefit. At 4.0+ mmol/L, the effects are dramatically amplified.

Reference: Nutrients. 2023;15(3):579 | Int J Mol Sci. 2024;25(2):698

2. Is therapeutic ketosis safe? Aren't high ketone levels dangerous?

Therapeutic ketosis (3–6 mmol/L) is safe under appropriate medical supervision and is fundamentally different from diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA).

Critical Distinction:
Condition Ketones Glucose pH
Therapeutic Ketosis 3–6 mmol/L 70–100 mg/dL Normal (7.35–7.45)
Ketoacidosis (DKA) >10 mmol/L >250 mg/dL Acidic (<7.3)

Why ketoacidosis doesn't occur in therapeutic ketosis:

  • Insulin (even basal levels) tightly regulates ketone production
  • When ketones rise, insulin secretion increases to prevent runaway production
  • DKA almost exclusively occurs in Type 1 diabetics who lack insulin

Historical context: Therapeutic ketosis is the metabolic state humans evolved to access during food scarcity. Our ancestors regularly entered 4–6 mmol/L ketosis during hunting/foraging gaps.

Reference: J Clin Invest. 2019;129(9):3419

3. Do I need to fast completely to reach 4.0+ mmol/L ketones?

No extended fasting required—though it's one way to reach these levels. SKLeTT achieves 4.0+ mmol/L through a specific nutrition strategy tailored to your individual needs.

How long it takes depends on:

  • Your adherence to the individualized protocol
  • Your age and muscle mass
  • Your metabolic history (e.g., diabetic vs. metabolically healthy)
  • Your body composition and BMI

The SKLeTT approach:

  • Carbohydrates: As close to zero as physiologically possible (some constraints apply)
  • Protein: Titrated to your ketone levels—varies significantly by individual
  • Fat intake: Determined by your BMI and body composition (high body fat % = less dietary fat needed; very lean individuals may need MCT oil supplementation)
  • Intermittent fasting: Recommended but tailored to your preference—you decide whether to eat once or twice daily

Timeline: Most people reach 4.0+ mmol/L by Week 3–4 with strict adherence, though this varies based on the factors above.

Why individualization matters: What works for someone with 15% body fat won't work for someone with 35%. Your specific macros are determined during consultation and adjusted daily based on your ketone response.

Section 2: The Science of BHB & Brain Benefits

4. What IS β-hydroxybutyrate (BHB) at the molecular level?

β-Hydroxybutyrate (BHB) is a four-carbon ketone body produced in the liver from fatty acid oxidation. It's the primary ketone in circulation during ketosis (70–80% of total ketones).

Molecular structure: CH₃-CHOH-CH₂-COOH (also called 3-hydroxybutyrate)

What makes BHB special:

  • Efficient fuel: Generates more ATP per oxygen molecule than glucose (~25% more efficient)
  • Signaling molecule: Acts beyond energy—BHB is an epigenetic regulator, HDAC inhibitor, and GPCR ligand
  • Crosses blood-brain barrier: Transported via MCT1 and MCT2 transporters on brain endothelium
  • Neuroprotective: Activates antioxidant pathways (Nrf2), reduces oxidative stress
Fun fact: BHB isn't technically a "ketone" (no C=O ketone group)—it's a β-hydroxy acid. But it's grouped with acetoacetate and acetone as "ketone bodies" for historical reasons.

Reference: Cell Metab. 2016;24(6):792–804

5. How does BHB improve cognition? What's the evidence?

BHB enhances cognition through multiple mechanisms:

1. Direct Energy Supply to Brain

  • Brain can derive 60–70% of energy from BHB during ketosis (vs. 0% on standard diet)
  • More efficient ATP production in mitochondria
  • Stabilizes brain energy levels (no glucose spikes/crashes)

2. BDNF Up-Regulation

BHB inhibits histone deacetylases (HDAC2/3), increasing BDNF expression 17–40%. BDNF supports:

  • Neuroplasticity (learning, memory formation)
  • Neuronal survival and growth
  • Synaptic function

Reference: eLife. 2016;5:e15092

3. Mitochondrial Biogenesis

BHB activates SIRT1 → AMPK → PGC-1α → new mitochondria. More mitochondria = more cellular energy capacity.

Reference: Int J Mol Sci. 2024;25(2):698

4. Reduced Neuroinflammation

BHB inhibits NLRP3 inflammasome, reducing IL-1β and IL-18 (pro-inflammatory cytokines linked to brain fog, depression).

Reference: Sci Rep. 2017;7:8917

Key Human Study: BHB infusion to 2.4 mmol/L improved working memory by ~17% in healthy adults (measured by digit span test).

Reference: Eur J Endocrinol. 2020;182(1):133–142

6. Does therapeutic ketosis improve sleep?

Yes—emerging evidence shows ketones improve sleep quality and architecture.

Key Findings:

  • Ketone ester supplementation (~3 mmol/L) increased sleep efficiency by 3%
  • Improved REM sleep restoration
  • Reduced nighttime wakefulness

Reference: Physiol Rep. 2023;11(20):e15829

Proposed mechanisms:

  • Stabilized brain energy: No hypoglycemia-induced arousals
  • Adenosine modulation: BHB may influence sleep-wake circuits
  • Reduced oxidative stress: Better mitochondrial function → less cellular stress → deeper sleep

Clinical observation: Many SKLeTT patients report sleep disturbances in Days 3–7 (adaptation phase), followed by significant improvement by Week 3–4.

7. Can therapeutic ketosis improve mood and reduce anxiety/depression?

Evidence suggests yes, though mechanisms are still being elucidated.

Research Findings:

  • Animal studies: BHB shows antidepressant-like effects via NLRP3 inhibition
  • Human case series: Improvement in treatment-resistant depression on ketogenic diets
  • Anxiety reduction: Some studies show reduced anxiety scores in epilepsy patients on keto

Reference: Sci Rep. 2017;7:8917 | Front Psychiatry. 2020;11:49

Proposed mechanisms:

  • GABA increase: Ketones enhance GABAergic tone (calming neurotransmitter)
  • Reduced inflammation: Neuroinflammation linked to depression
  • Mitochondrial restoration: Mood disorders associated with mitochondrial dysfunction
  • HPA axis regulation: Ketones may modulate stress response

Important caveat: Days 3–7 of ketosis often cause irritability and mood swings (adaptation). Mood benefits typically emerge Week 3+.

⚠️ Clinical Note: Patients with mood disorders should pursue therapeutic ketosis ONLY under psychiatric supervision. Do not discontinue psychiatric medications without physician guidance.

Section 3: Safety, Medical Supervision & Contraindications

9. Who should NOT attempt therapeutic ketosis? (Absolute contraindications)

Do NOT attempt SKLeTT if you have:

  • Type 1 diabetes: High risk of ketoacidosis without adequate insulin
  • History of pancreatitis: High-fat diet may trigger recurrence
  • Severe liver disease / cirrhosis: Impaired ketone metabolism
  • Severe kidney disease (GFR <30): Ketones and protein load may worsen function
  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding: Insufficient safety data; potential risks to fetal/infant development
  • Active eating disorder: Restrictive eating patterns may exacerbate disordered behaviors
  • Rare genetic disorders: Carnitine deficiency, fatty acid oxidation disorders, pyruvate kinase deficiency, porphyria

⚠️ If you have ANY of these conditions, therapeutic ketosis is NOT appropriate.

The following conditions require documentation and physician approval but are not absolute contraindications:

Kidney Stones:

  • Require baseline ultrasound documentation if history is known
  • Should be addressed/treated before starting protocol if active stones present
  • Managed within protocol through adequate hydration per protocol guidelines
  • Dr. B.D. has not observed complications in patients following hydration protocols
  • Physician approval required

Gallstones / Gallbladder Disease:

  • Require baseline ultrasound documentation
  • Should be addressed before starting if symptomatic
  • Fat intake (from animal protein throughout the diet) actually supports gallbladder function
  • Dr. B.D. has not observed gallbladder complications in protocol patients
  • Physician approval required

Gout:

  • NOT a contraindication—the protocol typically helps
  • Moderate protein intake (not high-protein diet) reduces uric acid issues
  • Elimination of inflammatory foods typically improves gout symptoms
  • Many patients reduce or discontinue gout medications under physician supervision
  • Dr. B.D. has not observed gout flares during the program

Moderate Kidney Disease (GFR 30–60):

  • Typically handled in-office only (New York patients)
  • Requires close lab monitoring throughout protocol
  • Dr. B.D. has observed GFR improvements in some cases (e.g., 50→100 over 3 months)

Children/Adolescents:

11. Why is physician involvement necessary? Can't I just follow the protocol myself?

Physician supervision is essential for two critical reasons:

1. SAFETY — Medication Adjustments Can Be Life-Threatening

Blood Pressure Medications:

  • BP drops 15–25 mmHg within 5–7 days
  • Continuing full-dose BP meds → dangerous hypotension, falls, syncope
  • Medications often reduced 50%+ or discontinued entirely

Diabetes Medications:

  • Blood glucose plummets within 48–72 hours
  • Sulfonylureas/insulin without dose adjustment → severe hypoglycemia (seizures, coma, potential death)
  • Must be stopped Day 1 with physician approval

Thyroid Medications:

  • Ketosis affects T3/T4 conversion
  • TSH monitoring and dose adjustments may be needed at 4–6 weeks

Other Concerns:

  • Electrolyte imbalances (especially with diuretics) can cause cardiac arrhythmias
  • Baseline labs identify hidden risks (kidney/liver dysfunction, cardiac issues)
  • Underlying conditions may be unmasked by metabolic stress

2. FEASIBILITY — It's Nearly Impossible Without Daily Guidance

In 30 years of practice, Dr. B.D. has found that reaching and maintaining 4.0+ mmol/L is nearly impossible without daily physician guidance. The rules are too strict and individualized.

Even with written instructions, most people:

  • Plateau at 2–3 mmol/L and can't break through
  • Make small errors that sabotage ketone production
  • Give up during Days 3–7 without support
  • Don't understand how to troubleshoot individual responses

Daily check-ins and real-time adjustments based on your ketone readings are essential for success.

The only way to reliably reach 4.0+ without physician guidance is extended water fasting—which isn't sustainable for 3 months and carries significant risks.

Section 4: Protocol Details & Practical Implementation

12. What if I've tried keto before and failed? Will SKLeTT be different?

Most "keto failures" are due to predictable, fixable issues—NOT lack of willpower.

Top Reasons People Fail (and How SKLeTT Fixes Them):

1. Electrolyte Deficiency (60% of early dropouts)

  • Problem: Insulin drop → kidney sodium/water loss → "keto flu" (fatigue, headache, cramps, brain fog)
  • SKLeTT Fix: Comprehensive electrolyte protocol from Day 1 (specific recommendations in consultation and protocol documents)

Reference: Nutrients. 2022;14(7):1410

2. Improper Protein Intake (20% of "non-responders")

  • Problem: Excess protein undergoes gluconeogenesis → glucose rises → insulin rises → ketones suppressed
  • SKLeTT Fix: Protein is titrated to YOUR ketone response; if you plateau, we adjust. You must follow the protocol and daily guidance.

3. Hidden Carbs / Voluntary Carb Intake (15%)

  • Problem: "Keto-friendly" products, restaurant meals with hidden sugar, underestimating vegetable carbs
  • SKLeTT Fix: No voluntary carbohydrate intake; whole-food focus; avoid processed "keto" snacks

4. Quitting in "The Valley" (Days 3–7) (30%)

  • Problem: Glycogen depleted but ketones still low; brain craves glucose; energy crashes; people quit thinking "this isn't working"
  • SKLeTT Fix: We set expectations upfront—Days 3–5 are the worst, Week 4 you feel great. Daily check-ins provide support through the difficult phase. You move through phases under Dr. B.D.'s guidance.

Bottom line: If you failed keto before, it was likely protocol design or lack of daily guidance—not you. SKLeTT provides the structure, accountability, and real-time adjustments that make success possible.

14. What happens when I transition OUT of therapeutic ketosis? Will I crash?

Transition happens under guidance over 4 weeks. With proper protocol, most people maintain benefits without "crashing."

Structured 4-Week Transition Protocol:

  • Week 1: Introduce non-starchy vegetables and continue protein
  • Week 2: Add nuts and seeds
  • Week 3: Add small portions of starch (≤1 cup daily)
  • Week 4: Add berries in moderation

Important Notes:

  • Ketones drop quickly: When you break ketosis (however you break it), ketones drop to zero within the first few days
  • Sugar/fruit not recommended long-term: Focus on metabolic flexibility rather than returning to high-sugar eating

What to Expect:

  • Weight: Expect 2–5 lbs water weight gain (glycogen + water restoration—not fat)
  • Energy: May feel slightly less "sharp" initially, but should stabilize within 2 weeks
  • Cravings: Gradual reintroduction helps avoid triggering sugar/carb addiction relapse

Long-Term Goal: Metabolic flexibility—your body regains the ability to efficiently burn both fat and carbohydrates, giving you dietary freedom while maintaining health benefits.
