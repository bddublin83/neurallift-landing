1. Protocol Overview

The SKLeTT Protocol is a physician-supervised therapeutic ketosis program designed to achieve and maintain blood ketone levels ≥4.0 mmol/L—the threshold at which significant neurological and metabolic benefits occur.

Unlike typical ketogenic diets that aim for mild ketosis (0.5–2.0 mmol/L), SKLeTT targets therapeutic ketosis (4.0–6.0 mmol/L), comparable to levels achieved through prolonged fasting but sustained through strategic nutrition and medical oversight.

Key Distinction: Therapeutic ketosis (4.0+ mmol/L) is NOT the same as diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA). DKA occurs when ketones exceed 10–15 mmol/L in the absence of insulin, combined with hyperglycemia. SKLeTT protocols maintain ketones in a safe, beneficial range with normal blood glucose control.

4. The Science Behind Therapeutic Ketosis

Why 4.0 mmol/L Matters

At ketone levels ≥4.0 mmol/L, β-hydroxybutyrate (BHB) triggers several key biological pathways:

1. Mitochondrial Biogenesis

BHB activates the SIRT1/AMPK/PGC-1α/FOXO3a pathway, triggering the production of new mitochondria—the cellular "power plants" that generate energy.

Reference: Int J Mol Sci. 2024;25(2):698

2. BDNF Up-Regulation

Brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) increases by 17–40% at therapeutic ketone levels, supporting neuroplasticity, learning, and memory.

Reference: eLife. 2016;5:e15092

3. Anti-Inflammatory Effects

BHB inhibits the NLRP3 inflammasome, reducing neuroinflammation associated with brain fog, depression, and neurodegenerative conditions.

Reference: Sci Rep. 2017;7:8917

4. Cognitive Enhancement

Studies show BHB infusion to 2.4 mmol/L improves working memory by ~17%. Higher ketone levels (4.0+) may amplify these effects.

Reference: Eur J Endocrinol. 2020;182(1):133–142

5. Sleep Quality Improvement

Ketone supplementation to ~3.0 mmol/L increases sleep efficiency by 3% and restores REM sleep patterns.

Reference: Physiol Rep. 2023;11(20):e15829

6. Protocol Phases

Phase 1: Preparation (Week Before Start)

  • Comprehensive medical evaluation (history, current medications, labs)
  • Baseline blood work: fasting glucose, HbA1c, lipid panel, electrolytes, liver/kidney function
  • Acquire blood ketone meter and test strips
  • Meal planning and electrolyte protocol education
  • Set realistic expectations and timeline

Phase 2: Induction (Weeks 1–2)

Goal: Achieve ketone levels ≥2.0 mmol/L and begin adaptation.

  • Strict macronutrient targets: 75–80% fat, 15–20% protein, <5% carbs
  • Total carbohydrates: <20 grams/day
  • Daily ketone and glucose testing (morning fasted, afternoon)
  • Aggressive electrolyte supplementation (see Electrolyte Protocol)
  • Check-in Days 3, 7, 14 (more frequent if on BP or diabetes medications)
Week 1–2 Reality Check: This is the hardest part. Expect fatigue, irritability, headaches, muscle cramps, sleep disruption, and intense carb cravings. Most people feel worse before they feel better. This is normal and temporary. The breakthrough happens Week 3+.

Phase 3: Optimization (Weeks 3–4)

Goal: Reach and stabilize at ≥4.0 mmol/L therapeutic ketosis.

  • Fine-tune protein intake (some people need to reduce further to reach 4.0+)
  • Possible addition of exogenous ketones (MCT oil, ketone esters) if needed
  • Energy and mental clarity typically improve significantly
  • Check-ins weekly

Phase 4: Maintenance (Weeks 5–12)

Goal: Sustain therapeutic ketosis for mitochondrial remodeling (6–12 weeks ideal).

  • Continue ketone monitoring (can reduce to 3–4x/week once stable)
  • Check-ins bi-weekly
  • Month 3: Repeat labs to assess metabolic changes
  • Most patients report peak benefits around Week 8–10

Phase 5: Transition & Long-Term Strategy (Post-Protocol)

  • Gradual carbohydrate re-introduction (increases of 10–20g/week)
  • Maintenance ketogenic or low-carb diet (many choose to stay 1.5–3.0 mmol/L)
  • Suggested cycle: 6–8 weeks therapeutic ketosis, 1–2x per year
  • Mitochondrial benefits persist 6–12 months after protocol ends

7. What to Expect: The Honest Timeline

Days 1–3: The Drop

Glycogen depletion. Fatigue, headache, irritability. Ketones rise to 0.5–1.5 mmol/L. You'll question why you started this.

Days 4–7: The Valley

The hardest part. Brain still craves glucose, ketone transport into brain not yet optimized. Ketones 1.5–3.0 mmol/L but you feel worse than Day 1. Intense cravings. Many quit here.

Days 8–14: The Breakthrough

Brain MCT1/MCT2 transporters up-regulate. Mental fog starts to lift. Ketones 3.0–4.0 mmol/L. Energy becomes more stable. You start to "feel it."

Weeks 3–4: The Ascent

Ketones stabilize ≥4.0 mmol/L. Mental clarity noticeably improves. Physical energy returns. Cravings diminish. Sleep quality may improve. You understand why people do this.

Weeks 5–12: The Plateau (Therapeutic Window)

Sustained therapeutic ketosis. Peak cognitive benefits. Mitochondrial biogenesis underway. Some report the best mental performance of their lives. This is where the transformation happens.

Critical Success Factor: Most failures happen in Days 4–7. Those who push through this "valley" and reach Week 3 have an 80%+ completion rate. The key is understanding that feeling terrible is part of the process, not a sign to quit.

8. Nutrition Guidelines

Macronutrient Targets

  • Fat: 75–80% of calories (prioritize quality: avocado, olive oil, nuts, fatty fish, grass-fed butter)
  • Protein: 15–20% of calories (0.8–1.0 g/kg body weight; excess protein can inhibit ketosis)
  • Carbohydrates: <20 grams total per day (NOT "net carbs"—count all carbs)

Foods to Emphasize

  • Fatty fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines)
  • Grass-fed meat, pasture-raised eggs
  • Avocados, olives, olive oil, coconut oil, MCT oil
  • Low-carb vegetables (leafy greens, broccoli, cauliflower, zucchini)
  • Nuts and seeds (macadamia, pecans, walnuts—in moderation)
  • Full-fat dairy (if tolerated): butter, ghee, hard cheeses

Foods to Avoid

  • All grains (wheat, rice, oats, corn)
  • All sugars (including honey, maple syrup, agave)
  • Most fruits (except small amounts of berries)
  • Starchy vegetables (potatoes, sweet potatoes, carrots, beets)
  • Legumes (beans, lentils, chickpeas)
  • Processed "keto" products with hidden carbs (sugar alcohols can spike insulin)
  • Alcohol (inhibits ketosis and fat metabolism)
The "Net Carbs" Myth: Many keto products subtract fiber and sugar alcohols to advertise low "net carbs." However, sugar alcohols like maltitol can still raise blood glucose and insulin in many people. SKLeTT counts TOTAL carbs to ensure true therapeutic ketosis.

9. Electrolyte Protocol

Electrolyte deficiency is the #1 cause of "keto flu" and early dropout. Insulin drops on keto, causing kidneys to excrete more sodium and water. This leads to a cascade of electrolyte losses.

Daily Targets (While in Ketosis)

  • Sodium: 3,000–5,000 mg/day (1–2 teaspoons of sea salt or pink Himalayan salt)
  • Potassium: 2,000–3,000 mg/day (food sources: avocado, spinach, salmon; supplement if needed)
  • Magnesium: 400–600 mg/day (magnesium glycinate or citrate; avoid oxide form)

Practical Implementation

  • Add 1/2 teaspoon sea salt to water, drink 2x daily
  • "Keto-ade" recipe: 1 liter water + 1/2 tsp sea salt + 1/4 tsp potassium chloride (NoSalt) + squeeze of lemon
  • Take magnesium supplement before bed (also helps sleep)
  • Salt food generously—don't fear salt on keto
Pro Tip: If you experience headaches, fatigue, muscle cramps, or dizziness in the first 2 weeks, increase sodium immediately. Many symptoms resolve within 30 minutes of drinking salted water.

Reference: Nutrients. 2022;14(7):1410

10. Exercise Guidance

Weeks 1–2: Go Easy

Your body is adapting to fat metabolism. High-intensity exercise will feel terrible and may impair ketone production.

  • Light walking: 20–30 minutes daily
  • Gentle yoga or stretching
  • Avoid HIIT, heavy lifting, or competitive sports

Weeks 3+: Gradual Re-Introduction

Once ketones are consistently ≥4.0 mmol/L and energy improves:

  • Resume moderate-intensity cardio (cycling, jogging, swimming)
  • Reintroduce resistance training gradually
  • Many athletes report better endurance and faster recovery in sustained ketosis

Reference: Physiol Rep. 2023;11(20):e15829

11. Monitoring & Tracking

Required Equipment

  • Blood Ketone Meter: Precision Xtra, Keto-Mojo, or equivalent (~$20–$50)
  • Ketone Test Strips: ~$1 per strip (budget $30–$60/month during induction)
  • Optional: Continuous glucose monitor (CGM) for real-time feedback on food responses

Testing Schedule

  • Weeks 1–2: Test 2x daily (morning fasted, afternoon)
  • Weeks 3–4: Test daily (morning fasted)
  • Weeks 5+: Test 3–4x per week once stable
Important: Urine ketone strips (Ketostix) are NOT accurate for monitoring therapeutic ketosis. They detect acetoacetate, not BHB, and become unreliable after the first week. ONLY blood ketone meters provide accurate data.

What to Track

  • Blood ketone levels (mmol/L)
  • Blood glucose (optional but recommended—aim for 70–90 mg/dL fasted)
  • Weight (weekly)
  • Subjective energy, mood, mental clarity (daily journal or 1–10 scale)
  • Sleep quality
  • Any side effects or symptoms

12. Medication Considerations

⚠️ CRITICAL: Medication Adjustments Required

Therapeutic ketosis significantly impacts blood pressure, blood sugar, and thyroid function. DO NOT attempt SKLeTT without physician supervision if you take any of the following medications:

Blood Pressure Medications

Expected Change: Systolic BP drops 15–25 mmHg within 5–7 days due to sodium/water loss and improved insulin sensitivity.

  • Risk: Orthostatic hypotension (dizziness upon standing), dangerously low BP
  • Action: BP meds often reduced by 50% on Day 1; some patients discontinue entirely by Week 2
  • Monitoring: Daily home BP checks; check-in Days 3, 5, 7

Diabetes Medications

Expected Change: Blood glucose drops rapidly—often within 48–72 hours.

  • High Risk Medications: Sulfonylureas (glipizide, glyburide), insulin
  • Risk: Severe hypoglycemia (blood sugar <55 mg/dL); can occur within 3 days
  • Action: Insulin/sulfonylureas often reduced 50–75% on Day 1; some patients stop entirely within 1 week under close supervision
  • Monitoring: Blood glucose testing 3–4x daily in first week; immediate physician contact if <70 mg/dL

Thyroid Medications

Expected Change: Ketosis can affect T3/T4 conversion and TSH levels.

  • Action: Recheck TSH at 4–6 weeks; dose adjustment may be needed
  • Monitoring: Track symptoms of hypo- or hyperthyroidism

Other Medications of Concern

  • Diuretics: Combined with keto's natural diuretic effect, may cause dangerous electrolyte imbalance
  • Lithium: Ketosis can alter lithium levels; requires close monitoring
  • SGLT2 Inhibitors (Jardiance, Farxiga): Increased risk of euglycemic ketoacidosis—generally contraindicated

13. Contraindications & Safety

Absolute Contraindications (Do Not Attempt SKLeTT)

  • Type 1 diabetes (high ketoacidosis risk)
  • History of pancreatitis
  • Severe liver disease or cirrhosis
  • Severe kidney disease (GFR <30)
  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding
  • Active eating disorder (anorexia, bulimia)
  • Rare genetic disorders: carnitine deficiency, fatty acid oxidation disorders, pyruvate kinase deficiency

Relative Contraindications (Proceed with Extreme Caution)

  • History of kidney stones (ketosis may increase risk)
  • Gallbladder disease or history of gallstones
  • Gout (ketones compete with uric acid excretion)
  • Osteoporosis (long-term keto may affect bone density; ensure adequate vitamin D, K2, calcium)
  • Children under 18 (growth considerations; specialized supervision required)

Side Effects (Common, Usually Temporary)

  • "Keto flu" (headache, fatigue, irritability): Days 3–7, resolves with electrolytes
  • Constipation: Increase fiber from low-carb vegetables, magnesium citrate
  • Bad breath (acetone): Temporary, resolves after Week 2–3
  • Muscle cramps: Electrolyte deficiency—increase sodium, potassium, magnesium
  • Sleep disturbances: Common Week 1–2; improves Week 3+
  • Increased urination: Normal due to diuretic effect and glycogen depletion

⚠️ Stop Protocol & Contact Physician Immediately If:

  • Ketones exceed 8.0 mmol/L (especially if diabetic)
  • Severe nausea, vomiting, or abdominal pain
  • Symptoms of hypoglycemia (shakiness, confusion, rapid heartbeat) and glucose <55 mg/dL
  • Symptoms of severe dehydration (dark urine, dizziness, confusion)
  • Chest pain, shortness of breath, or irregular heartbeat

14. Frequently Asked Questions

1. Is therapeutic ketosis safe? Won't ketones harm me?

Short answer: Therapeutic ketosis (3–6 mmol/L) is safe for healthy individuals under medical supervision. It is NOT the same as diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA).

The distinction:

  • Therapeutic ketosis: Ketones 3–6 mmol/L, normal blood glucose (70–100 mg/dL), normal pH. This is a natural metabolic state similar to fasting.
  • Ketoacidosis (DKA): Ketones >10 mmol/L, HIGH blood glucose (>250 mg/dL), acidic pH (<7.3). This is a medical emergency, almost always occurring in uncontrolled Type 1 diabetes when insulin is absent.

Your body tightly regulates ketone production in the presence of adequate insulin. SKLeTT monitoring ensures you stay in the safe, beneficial range.

Reference: Nutrients. 2023;15(3):579

2. Do I have to fast completely to reach 4.0+ mmol/L ketones?

No. While prolonged fasting (2–5 days) reliably produces ketones >4.0 mmol/L, SKLeTT achieves the same levels through strategic nutrition without extended fasting.

How we do it:

  • Very low carbohydrate intake (<20g/day)
  • Moderate protein (excess protein converts to glucose via gluconeogenesis)
  • High fat intake (75–80% of calories)
  • Optional: MCT oil or exogenous ketones to boost levels
  • Some patients use intermittent fasting (16:8 or 18:6) to accelerate results, but it's not required

Most people reach 4.0+ mmol/L by Week 3–4 on the full protocol without extended fasting.

3. How long until I see results?

Honest answer: It depends on what "results" you're looking for.

Timeline:

  • Week 1–2: You'll feel WORSE (fatigue, brain fog, irritability). This is adaptation, not failure.
  • Week 3: Mental clarity starts to improve. Energy stabilizes. Ketones reach 3.0–4.0 mmol/L.
  • Week 4–6: Most people report noticeable cognitive improvement—better focus, faster thinking, improved mood.
  • Week 8–12: Peak benefits. Mitochondrial biogenesis is underway. Many describe this as "the best my brain has felt in years."

Long-term: Mitochondrial remodeling takes 6–12 weeks. Benefits persist for 6–12 months after returning to normal diet.

Reference: Int J Mol Sci. 2024;25(2):698

4. Can I do this on my own without a doctor?

Not recommended if you:

  • Take ANY medications (BP, diabetes, thyroid, diuretics, etc.)
  • Have any chronic health conditions
  • Are over 50 years old (higher risk of undiagnosed conditions)
  • Have never done a ketogenic diet before

Why physician oversight matters:

  • Medications may need rapid adjustment (BP meds often cut by 50% within days)
  • Diabetes medications can cause dangerous hypoglycemia within 48–72 hours
  • Baseline labs identify hidden risks (kidney function, liver function, electrolytes)
  • Close monitoring catches problems early (electrolyte imbalance, excessive ketone elevation)

If you're young, healthy, and on no medications, you can attempt nutritional ketosis (1.5–3.0 mmol/L) independently. But therapeutic ketosis (4.0+) carries higher risks and should be supervised.

5. Will I lose muscle on the SKLeTT Protocol?

Short answer: Not if you do it correctly.

How to preserve muscle:

  • Adequate protein: 0.8–1.0 g/kg body weight (more for athletes). Too much protein inhibits ketosis; too little causes muscle loss.
  • Resistance training: Maintain (don't start new heavy programs during Week 1–2, but continue existing routines by Week 3+).
  • Sufficient calories: Don't combine therapeutic ketosis with severe calorie restriction.
  • Electrolytes: Muscle function requires adequate sodium, potassium, magnesium.

Research shows: Well-formulated ketogenic diets preserve lean mass comparable to higher-carb diets when protein and training are adequate.

Reference: J Int Soc Sports Nutr. 2017;14:16

6. What if I have diabetes? Can I still do SKLeTT?

Type 1 Diabetes: Absolute contraindication. Do not attempt. High risk of ketoacidosis.

Type 2 Diabetes: Often excellent candidates—BUT require very close supervision.

Why Type 2 diabetics benefit:

  • Ketogenic diets dramatically improve insulin sensitivity
  • Blood glucose typically drops significantly within 48–72 hours
  • HbA1c improvements of 1–2 points common over 3 months
  • Many patients reduce or eliminate diabetes medications

Critical safety measures for Type 2 diabetics:

  • Baseline HbA1c, fasting glucose, kidney function (GFR) required
  • Medication adjustment BEFORE starting (especially sulfonylureas, insulin)
  • Blood glucose testing 3–4x daily in first 2 weeks
  • Close physician contact Days 1, 3, 5, 7, 14
  • Immediate reporting of hypoglycemia symptoms

Reference: Diabetes Ther. 2018;9(3):1263–1294

7. How do I measure my ketone levels? Are urine strips accurate?

Blood ketone meter = GOLD STANDARD.

Why blood testing is necessary for SKLeTT:

  • Measures β-hydroxybutyrate (BHB)—the primary ketone responsible for therapeutic effects
  • Accurate throughout all stages of ketosis
  • Essential for monitoring therapeutic range (4.0+ mmol/L)

Urine ketone strips (Ketostix):

  • Measure acetoacetate (a different ketone)
  • Only semi-reliable in first 1–2 weeks
  • Become unreliable once "keto-adapted" (kidneys stop excreting ketones)
  • Cannot accurately measure levels >3 mmol/L
  • Verdict: NOT suitable for SKLeTT Protocol

Breath ketone meters:

  • Measure acetone (a third ketone)
  • Moderate correlation with blood ketones but less precise
  • Useful for tracking trends, not for precise therapeutic monitoring
  • Verdict: Optional adjunct, but not a replacement for blood testing

Recommended meters: Precision Xtra, Keto-Mojo, or equivalent. Test strips cost ~$1 each.
